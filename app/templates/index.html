    How it works

    Metadata & provenance

    Reads EXIF, XMP and IPTC data, C2PA Content Credentials (used by OpenAI, Google, Adobe, Microsoft), China's AIGC labels (ByteDance), and generation parameters left by Stable Diffusion tools. This is the only way to name the specific platform with confidence.

    Frequency spectrum

    Generators up-sample images in fixed steps, leaving periodic peaks in the Fourier spectrum that real photographs don't have.

    Noise & texture

    Camera sensors add fine grain everywhere. Generated images are often unnaturally clean in flat areas and render surfaces with a smooth, waxy look.

    Camera sensor traces

    Real cameras interpolate colours through a Bayer filter, leaving a 2×2 periodic pattern. Finding it is strong evidence of a genuine capture.

    Facial consistency

    For each face: do the reflections in both eyes match, are the eyes alike, and does the skin share the grain of the rest of the photo?

    Error level analysis

    Re-compresses the image to reveal regions with a different editing history, a sign of compositing or AI in-painting.

    Each check produces a log-likelihood ratio; they're combined into one probability. Pixel checks alone are capped so they never claim near-certainty, while signed provenance metadata can. Note that Anthropic's Claude models don't natively generate images, so they're detected only through metadata references.

    Results are probabilistic estimates, not proof. Screenshots, resizing and re-compression weaken the evidence.
